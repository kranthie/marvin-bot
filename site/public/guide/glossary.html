Abduction
Moving a limb away from the body's midline (positive roll on Marvin Bot).

Accelerometer
A sensor that measures gravity's pull plus the body's own acceleration, so at rest it tells which way is down.

Action
What the policy outputs at each step. For Marvin Bot, one number per joint: a target angle offset from the standing pose, which a fast PD loop in each motor then tries to reach.

Action chunk
A short sequence of future actions output at once by a policy, executed while the next chunk is computed.

Action scale
The factor converting the policy's output into a joint-angle offset from the stance; 0.5 rad per unit for Marvin Bot.
On: 16 Reinforcement learning for walking
Active stereo
Stereo depth with an infrared projector adding a dot pattern, so that textureless surfaces such as plain floors can still be matched.
On: 11 Sensors and compute
Actuator
A motor with its gearbox, encoder and driver electronics in one unit.

Actuator bench
A test rig for single actuators. It measures torque, speed, heating and communication timing before many units are bought.

Actuator delay
Time between a command being computed and the motor acting on it; Marvin Bot's training randomises 0-10 ms.
On: 15 PD control, gains and armature
Actuator network
A small neural network trained on bench data to predict an actuator's torque from recent commands and motion, used in simulation instead of an ideal motor model.
On: 20 Sim-to-real and system identification
Adaptive sampling
Starting training episodes more often at the parts of the clips where the policy has been failing.
On: 17 Motion tracking: Kimodo, retargeting and BeyondMimic
Advantage
During training: how much better an action turned out than expected. A second network (the critic) predicts how well things should go from a situation; if the action did better than that, it gets a positive advantage and becomes more likely next time.

AHRS
Attitude and heading reference system: an IMU (motion sensor) with its own built-in processing, so it reports the body's tilt and heading directly instead of raw rotation rates and accelerations.
On: 11 Sensors and compute
Aliasing
When a signal is sampled too slowly, fast vibrations masquerade as slow ones; sampling fast and averaging prevents it.
On: 11 Sensors and compute
Anchor body
The body (usually the torso) whose pose is tracked globally; the other bodies are compared relative to it, so drift in position is not counted as pose error.
On: 17 Motion tracking: Kimodo, retargeting and BeyondMimic
Ankle strategy
Balancing by shifting pressure between heel and toes with ankle torque, without stepping.
On: 02 Balance: centre of mass, support polygon, ZMP and capture point
Anthropometry
The measurement of human body dimensions. Tables give segment lengths as fractions of standing height.
On: 07 Mass budget and proportions
Arbitration
How CAN decides who talks when two devices start at once: each sends its identifier number bit by bit, and the device with the lower number wins and carries on, while the other waits. Nothing is garbled.
On: 10 CAN buses and bandwidth
Armature
The motor rotor's inertia as felt at the joint (the reflected inertia: rotor inertia times the gear ratio squared). Simulators add it to the joint's own inertia; Marvin Bot's values are placeholders until the phase 2 bench measures them.
On: 15 PD control, gains and armature, 20 Sim-to-real and system identification
Asymmetric actor-critic
A training trick: the policy that will run on the robot (the actor) sees only what real sensors can measure, while the helper network that grades it during training (the critic) is allowed to see simulator-only facts such as the true speed or the exact terrain.
On: 16 Reinforcement learning for walking
Back-EMF
The voltage a spinning motor generates against its supply, proportional to speed. It sets the top speed for a given battery voltage.
On: 04 Actuators: quasi-direct drive, torque, speed and heat
Backdrivability
How easily the output can be turned by an external force. Backdrivable actuators absorb impacts and can sense torque from motor current.
On: 04 Actuators: quasi-direct drive, torque, speed and heat
Baseline
The distance between a stereo camera's two cameras; a longer baseline gives smaller depth error at range.
On: 11 Sensors and compute
Bending moment
The turning effect of a load about a section of a beam: force times distance, in N·m.
On: 08 Structure and code-CAD
Bias
A slowly drifting offset in a sensor's reading; integrated gyro bias makes an angle estimate drift without limit.
On: 11 Sensors and compute
Bill of materials (BOM)
The complete list of parts, quantities and prices needed to build a product. Marvin Bot's is the spreadsheet bom/bom.xlsx.

Bit stuffing
A CAN rule: after five identical bits in a row, the sender inserts one opposite bit so receivers can keep their clocks in step. It means a frame's length depends slightly on the data it carries.
On: 10 CAN buses and bandwidth
BMS
Battery management system: electronics that watch every cell's voltage and temperature, balance them and disconnect the pack on a fault.
On: 09 Power and battery
Bought part
A part bought finished, such as an actuator, camera or bearing. Its cost is the price on a listing.
On: 21 Bill of materials and costs
Bus load
The fraction of time a bus is busy transmitting frames.
On: 10 CAN buses and bandwidth
C-rate
Current divided by a cell's capacity: 1C empties it in an hour, 10C in six minutes.
On: 09 Power and battery
CAN bus
Controller Area Network: a two-wire differential bus on which every node hears every frame and identifiers set priority; RobStride actuators use classic CAN at 1 Mbit/s.

CAN FD
CAN with flexible data rate: the data phase runs faster than arbitration and frames carry up to 64 bytes.
On: 10 CAN buses and bandwidth
Capture point
The spot on the ground where the robot would have to step (or put its centre of pressure) to come to a complete stop. The faster the body is moving, the further ahead that spot is.

Carrier board
The board a compute module such as a Jetson plugs into. It provides the connectors and power. A developer kit is a module already on a carrier board.

Centre of mass
The mass-weighted average position of all a robot's parts; gravity acts as if all the weight were there. Balance depends on keeping it over the feet.

Centre of pressure
The single point where the ground's distributed push on the soles can be treated as acting.

A test input whose frequency sweeps from low to high, used to excite a system across its frequency range.

Class-T fuse
A fast, current-limiting fuse with a high DC interrupting rating, used to break a hard short of a lithium pack safely.

Closed kinematic chain
A set of links and joints that forms a loop, so the joint angles are not independent; most simulators need an extra constraint to handle it.

Code-CAD
Writing mechanical parts as programs that build solids from primitives and boolean operations, so they are parametric, diffable and can feed other tools directly.

Coefficient of friction
μ, the ratio of the largest sideways friction force to the pressing force at a contact; soft rubber has a higher μ than hard plastic.

Coefficient of restitution
Ratio of rebound speed to impact speed; 0 means no bounce, 1 a perfectly elastic bounce.

Collision geometry
Simple shapes the physics engine uses to detect contact; kept simple because contact checks run every step.
On: 13 The robot description: one source of truth
Collision sweep
Moving each joint through its range in software and checking for overlaps between parts, to set joint limits and find packaging errors.
On: 08 Structure and code-CAD
Complementary filter
A simple way to estimate tilt from an IMU: trust the gyroscope for quick changes (it is smooth but slowly drifts) and the accelerometer's sense of "down" for the long-term average (it is noisy but does not drift).
On: 11 Sensors and compute
Constraint solver
The part of a physics engine that works out all the contact and joint forces at once each step, so that feet do not sink into the floor and joints stay connected. PGS, TGS and MuJoCo's Newton solver are different methods for this.

Contactor
A heavy-duty relay that switches the main battery current; on Marvin Bot the e-stop chain opens it.

Control hierarchy
Stacking control loops at different rates, each slower loop setting targets for a faster one below it: VLA at a few hertz, policy at 50 Hz, joint PD and current loops at kilohertz.

Copper loss
Heat made by current flowing through the motor's copper windings. It grows with the current squared, so doubling the torque roughly quadruples the heat.

Coulomb friction
A constant resisting torque that opposes motion regardless of speed, from gear teeth and seals.
On: 20 Sim-to-real and system identification
Coupled joint limit
A joint limit that depends on another joint's angle; on Marvin Bot the shoulder roll may go below 0 degrees only with the arm swung forward.

Crank
A short arm fixed to a motor shaft; its tip moves on a circle and pushes or pulls a rod.
On: 06 The parallel-linkage ankle

Training that starts with easy tasks (flat ground, slow speeds) and makes them harder as the policy improves.

Cycloidal drive
A gearbox in which a lobed disc rolls inside a ring of pins, sharing load over many lobes; compact and shock-tolerant.

Damped least squares
A way to solve inverse kinematics step by step: nudge every joint in the direction that moves the foot (or hand) towards its target, with a little damping so the steps stay small and stable near awkward poses where the leg is straight.

Damping (kd)
The "D" setting of a PD controller (in N·m·s/rad): how strongly a joint pushes back against moving fast, like a door closer. More damping means less wobble but slower motion.

Damping mode
All joints commanded to resist motion like dampers, with no position target, so a falling robot crumples instead of thrashing.

Damping ratio
A single number that says how a spring-like joint settles: below 1 it overshoots and wobbles, at exactly 1 it arrives as fast as possible without overshooting, above 1 it creeps in slowly. Computed as kd / (2 sqrt(kp J)).
On: 15 PD control, gains and armature
DC-DC converter
An electronic circuit that converts one DC voltage to another, such as 48 V to 12 V.
On: 09 Power and battery
Decoupled control
Splitting a humanoid's control into a learned lower-body balance/locomotion policy and a separate arm controller such as IK.

Several independent safety layers, so a fault one layer misses is caught by the next.

A number γ between 0 and 1 that sets how much future rewards count; 0.99 at 50 Hz means the policy looks about 2 s ahead.

How far a point shifts between a stereo camera's two images, in pixels; depth is focal length times baseline divided by disparity.

Mass far from the joint that swings a limb, such as motors at the ankle. It costs inertia as the square of its distance.

How heavy a moving part of the robot feels to whatever it hits. A long arm swung from the shoulder can feel lighter or heavier at the hand than its real mass, depending on how its mass is spread out.
