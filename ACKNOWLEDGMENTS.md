# Acknowledgments

Marvin Bot is designed from scratch, but it learns from many open projects, published specifications and tools. This file credits them. Nothing from them is copied into this repository unless a line below says so and the licence allows it.

## Humanoid designs (ideas and reference figures)

- **Asimov 1**: https://github.com/menloresearch/asimov-1 (hardware CERN-OHL-S-2.0, software GPL-2.0). The main reference for the overall layout: a 1.2 m, 35 kg, 25-DOF humanoid with 6-DOF legs, 5-DOF arms, waist yaw and a 2-DOF neck, a two-motor parallel-linkage ankle, and per-joint torque classes. Used for ideas and sizing only; no Asimov CAD, drawings or code are used here.
- **Roboto Origin**: https://github.com/Roboparty/roboto_origin (GPL-3.0 at the top level; its hardware, description and training modules keep their own licences, listed in its LICENSES.md). An open 1.25 m, 34 kg, 23-DOF humanoid; reference for an all-machined structure, two-motor ankles with a parallel linkage, an off-the-shelf 48 V pack, a published BOM and per-joint actuator gains for Isaac Lab. Used for ideas and reference figures only.
- **Berkeley Humanoid Lite** (UC Berkeley Hybrid Robotics): https://github.com/HybridRobotics/Berkeley-Humanoid-Lite and arXiv:2504.17249. Lessons on low-cost actuation, printed structures that route load through metal fasteners, and sim-to-real training.
- **ToddlerBot** (Stanford University): https://github.com/hshi74/toddlerbot. Lessons on zero-point calibration and data collection for a low-cost open humanoid.
- Open RobStride actuator drivers (https://github.com/kscalelabs/actuator) and an all-RobStride open humanoid (https://github.com/kscalelabs/kbot; hardware CERN-OHL-S, software GPL-3.0) were looked at for ideas. No code or CAD from them is used: Marvin Bot's RobStride driver is written from RobStride's manuals.
- **Unitree Robotics**: published specifications of the G1 and R1 (https://www.unitree.com) used as size, mass and joint-range benchmarks; the cost-driven design idea of actuator housings carrying structural load. The G1 also appears through open-source tools that support it (Isaac Lab's and mjlab's G1 tasks, ProtoMotions' G1 retargeter). No Unitree CAD, images, logos or code are used.
- **Booster Robotics**: published specifications of the K1 (https://www.booster.tech) as a size benchmark, and its robots as an example of the general idea that structural limb shells and exposed actuator caps can make a robot look finished without a separate cover. No Booster CAD, images, logos or code are used.

## Software and models

- **NVIDIA Isaac Lab** (https://github.com/isaac-sim/IsaacLab, BSD-3-Clause), **Isaac Sim** (NVIDIA licence, installed by the user, not redistributed), **Newton** and **Isaac ROS**: training environments and the onboard perception stack. The walking tasks' structure and rewards, the PPO settings, the terrain generator config and `DelayedDCMotor` in `sim/isaaclab/` are adapted from Isaac Lab's G1 velocity tasks, rough-terrain config, quadruped foot-clearance term and `DelayedPDActuator`; those files keep Isaac Lab's notice (`LICENSES/BSD-3-Clause-IsaacLab.txt`).
- **NVIDIA Isaac GR00T N1.7** (https://github.com/NVIDIA/Isaac-GR00T) and **GR00T-WholeBodyControl** (https://github.com/NVlabs/GR00T-WholeBodyControl): the planned vision-language-action model and the decoupled whole-body control approach.
- **MuJoCo** (Google DeepMind, https://github.com/google-deepmind/mujoco, Apache-2.0): the simulation cross-checks and renders.
- **rsl_rl** (ETH Zurich Robotic Systems Lab, https://github.com/leggedrobotics/rsl_rl, BSD-3-Clause): PPO training, used as a library.
- **ProtoMotions** (NVIDIA, https://github.com/NVlabs/ProtoMotions, Apache-2.0): the motion pipeline in `sim/motion/` runs its keypoint extraction, PyRoki retargeter (G1 version, with Marvin Bot's link map and scaling), converters, motion library and motion-tracking training. Its sample human motions, on NVIDIA's SOMA skeleton, are from the BONES-SEED dataset: Motion Data by Bones Studio (https://bones.studio), used under the BONES-SEED License, which grants rights only to non-profit academic institutions and companies under $1M annual revenue; it is not redistributed here, and nothing derived from it is published.
- **PyRoki** (https://github.com/chungmin99/pyroki, MIT): the kinematics optimiser behind the retargeter.
- **BeyondMimic** (https://github.com/HybridRobotics/whole_body_tracking, arXiv:2508.08241): the motion-tracking formulation ProtoMotions' tracker follows. **Kimodo** (NVIDIA, https://github.com/nv-tlabs/kimodo; weights under the NVIDIA Open Model License, text encoder Meta's gated Llama 3) and **GMR** (https://github.com/YanjieZe/GMR): planned motion generation and an alternative retargeter.
- **NVIDIA Cosmos** (world models and open physical-AI datasets), **GR00T-Mimic** and **SONIC** (GR00T-WholeBodyControl): planned sources of synthetic manipulation data and of a general whole-body tracker.
- Papers behind the planned skills in `docs/roadmap.md`: HoST (arXiv 2502.08378) and arXiv 2502.12152 (getting up), TWIST (arXiv 2505.02833) and HOMIE (arXiv 2502.13013) (teleoperation and loco-manipulation), Hiking in the Wild (arXiv 2601.07718) and PIM (arXiv 2411.14386) (stairs from depth), PhysHSI (arXiv 2510.11072) (carrying and sitting), PAiD (arXiv 2602.05310) and the RoboCup 2025 soccer work (kicking). Ideas only; no code from them is used.
- **mjlab** (https://github.com/mujocolab/mjlab, Apache-2.0): its Unitree G1 velocity task showed the anti-sway terms used in the walking rewards (an upright term and tight hip and ankle roll while walking, no single-support reward).
- **manifold3d** (https://github.com/elalish/manifold, Apache-2.0) and **trimesh** (https://github.com/mikedh/trimesh, MIT): the code-CAD in `cad/` (solids, booleans, meshes); **fast-simplification** (MIT) decimates the hand meshes. **NumPy**, **PyYAML**, **Pillow**, **ONNX Runtime**, **matplotlib**, **imageio** and **pytest** for the tools and checks; **python-can** (LGPL-3.0, optional, bench only) for the CAN bus; **PyTorch**, **JAX** and **yourdfpy** through the motion pipeline's upstream tools.
- **bd_warehouse** (https://github.com/gumyr/bd_warehouse, Apache-2.0) and **build123d**: planned sources of parametric fasteners, bearings and code-CAD.

- **Claude** and **Claude Code** (Anthropic): the project is developed with them, from research and design reviews to the code-CAD, simulation and training code and the documentation; commits they contributed to carry a `Co-Authored-By: Claude` line.

## Component specifications

- **RobStride** official manuals and specifications: https://github.com/RobStride/Product_Information. Actuator figures in `description/actuators.yaml` come from these manuals unless marked otherwise.
- **Feetech** STS3250 datasheet (neck and wrist pitch servos) and SCS0009 figures (the hands' finger servos).
- **AmazingHand** by **Pollen Robotics**: https://github.com/pollen-robotics/AmazingHand (mechanical design CC-BY-4.0, code Apache-2.0). Marvin Bot's hands are AmazingHand kits (bought from Seeed Studio, SKUs 100062181 and 100063642). The hand meshes in `description/third_party/amazing_hand/` are derived from its CAD and MuJoCo models (merged, simplified, re-framed; see the README there) and stay under CC-BY-4.0; `tools/build_hand_mesh.py` rebuilds them. Its published mass and wrist-interface bolt pattern set the hand mount in `cad/arm.py`. No AmazingHand code is used.
- **Orbbec** Gemini 335L product documentation (head depth camera).
- **NVIDIA** Jetson AGX Orin developer kit (P3730) model: its outline and port positions place the computer in `cad/electrical.py`; the model is used locally and not redistributed.
- Human body proportions for the arm lengths (forearm 0.146 and hand 0.108 of height): D. A. Winter, *Biomechanics and Motor Control of Human Movement*, after Drillis and Contini (1966).
